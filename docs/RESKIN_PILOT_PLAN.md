# Re-skin pilot plan (2026-11-05)

## Why

Every class splits into two branches at level 25 (decided 2026-11-05; the Rogue becomes Assassin + Spellblade, the Ranger is gone). At level 25 the character's LOOK changes, but every animation stays exactly the same frame by frame: the same poses, the same hand marks, paths, fps and effects. Each class therefore needs three looks (base, branch A, branch B) over the same animations.

**The question the pilot answers:** can ChatGPT or Gemini redraw an existing animation in a new look closely enough that the old marks, paths and effects still fit, with only a small automatic correction? Our experience says "close, not exact": the generators redraw a sheet instead of editing it, drift in size (the Shadow Shiv sheet came out 1.23 x too big, some Abyssal panels' heads 15% small) and in details from frame to frame (which shows as shimmer when played).

## Sizing (measured on the Rogue's strips)

About 65 unique images per look when counted per animation (idle 6, run 6, hit 6, death 6, dodge 6, Umbral Shroud 6, Abyssal Backstab 7, Winter's Kiss 6, Shadow Shiv 4, Static Stiletto 4, Permafrost Execution 8). Some are shared between animations (the Stiletto re-uses the Shiv's frames, the Abyssal Backstab the Umbral Shroud's first two, every skill ends on the idle's first frame), so about 55-60 DISTINCT images per look, roughly ten sheets of six if every sheet came out right the first time. Two new looks per class: about 115 images per class. That is why the pilot measures drift first and the tools do the repeating work.

### Step 2: the tools (BUILT 2026-11-05, in `source/tools/`; tested on the Rogue's strips with a fake drifted re-skin)

1. `make_pose_guides.py <strip> [--share-with <strip> ...]`: finds the strip's unique images and writes flat grey SILHOUETTE guides (smoothed, no folds; a thin ground line where she stands; a RED dot at the 'main' hand mark and a BLUE dot at the 'off' mark) as 3 x 2 guide sheets and single guides, plus `frame_map.json` (which image sits at which position, which are shared) and a ready `prompt.txt` (attach the new look's reference image and one guide sheet, fill in the look's description). `--share-with` leaves out the images that another animation re-uses (the idle's first frame ends every skill, the Umbral Shroud's first two frames start the Abyssal Backstab, also across different cell heights), because they come from that animation's re-skin. Output: `reskin/<Character>_<animation>/` (made now for the Rogue's idle, Permafrost Execution and Abyssal Backstab).
2. `check_reskin.py --orig <strip> --new <cut strip(s)> --map <frame_map.json> [--align out.png]`: per frame, the best scale and shift that fit the new silhouette onto the original's (soft overlap of the two blurred silhouettes), what was drifting as delivered (height ratio, feet, centre) and a verdict: OK / FIXED (fits after the correction but had drifted: scale off the animation's median by more than 4%, feet off by more than 5 px on a standing frame, centre off by more than 12 px) / BAD (overlap below 0.75 even at its best: wrong pose, draw it again). The correction uses the animation's median scale for frames within 2% of it (no jitter from noise in the fit) and their own scale otherwise, puts standing frames back on the ground line, and `--align` writes the corrected frames. The POSE test uses the FRONT edge of the figure (the rightmost pixel of every row: head, chest, front arm, front leg, the side facing the enemy; the back is left out because a new look has another cape, hair or cloak there): BAD when it is on average more than 6% of the figure's height from the original's, or when the whole-silhouette overlap is below 0.60 (a right pose in a new look scored about 0.7, wrong poses about 0.5). It writes `check_report.json` and overlay sheets (the original's outline in red and its hand-mark dots over the new frame, as delivered on top and corrected below): the fists of the new frame must sit on the dots. Tested: scale and shift were recovered to within about 1% / 2 px; the original's hard-mask overlap after the correction was 0.92-0.985 even with an outline 7 px fatter; frames drawn in the wrong order scored about 0.5 (BAD).
   LIMITS: (a) similar poses cannot be told apart by their silhouette: two frames that differ only in the arms (the Permafrost Execution's settle and crossed-arms focus) score alike, so the overlay dots are the real fist check (a skin-colour fist finder is possible later); (b) an idle's six frames are nearly identical (a breathing loop): small errors show as flicker, look at the played strip.
3. `reskin_strip.py --orig <strip> --map <frame_map.json> --new <aligned strip> [--shared <new strip> ...] [--override UID=STRIP:FRAME] --stem <name>_strip --out-dir <dir> [--char-name <name>]`: puts every new image back where the original had it (repeats for timing restored, shared frames taken from the new version of the other animation, a 512 cell put on the bottom of a tall one) and copies the hand marks (animation and character renamed) and the path file (fps, hit frame, x, y). Tested round trip: same repeat structure, marks and path copied.
4. `transfer_motion.py --orig <original idle> --new <aligned frames> --out <strip>` (added after the first real result): for a BREATHING IDLE the generator draws six near-identical panels (no breathing, a wobbling cape, flickering shading); this takes one generated frame and warps it with the smoothed optical flow of the original idle's frames, so the loop moves exactly like the original (the marks and timing fit) and nothing flickers.
(`reskin_lib.py` holds the shared helpers.)

### Step 4: cut, check, correct (I do)

Cut with `slice_keypose_sheet.py`, run `check_reskin.py`, apply `--align`, rebuild with `reskin_strip.py`, and look at the new strips in the test page beside the originals (the weapons must sit in the fists, the strip must not shimmer).

### Step 5: decide

Tolerances after the automatic correction: the head within 4% of the original's size, the feet within 5 px, the fists within 8 px (the marks' tolerance). The pilot passes when at least 80% of the frames are inside the tolerances and the played strips do not visibly shimmer. Then the same workflow is used for every animation and every class. If sheet (A) and single images (B) differ clearly, we use the better one.

If it fails: (1) the same pilot with the other generator (Gemini and ChatGPT behave differently), (2) generate one frame at a time with a single guide (method B) instead of sheets, (3) redraw the failing frames by hand or with an artist. (A mask or palette re-skin is not an option: the branch looks are completely different.)
