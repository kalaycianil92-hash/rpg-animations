# Monsters

Started 2026-11-07. The monsters of the five acts (`docs/STAGES.md`): slimes, goblins, wolves (dark and holy), ogres, orcs. Until now the test page has only a green block labelled GOBLIN. This file holds the decisions and the state; the prompts live in each monster's `source/` folder and are summarised in `docs/PROMPTS.md` when they have been used.

## Decisions (2026-11-07, the user agreed to the proposals and added two things)

- **Default animations of every monster: idle, attack, hit, death and dodge** (the dodge was added by the user; it is a hop or a slide back with a path, like the characters').
- **Every monster also gets SPECIAL animations** besides the default attack (the user's note). Not designed yet; they come after the defaults. For the elemental slimes the natural ones are the status skills the page already draws (Burn, Chilled, Frozen, Shocked, Magic Def reduced, Blinded); suggestions only, to be agreed per monster.
- **Drawn facing right** like the characters; the page mirrors the monster (as it does for the placeholder).
- **Weapons are painted into the monster's art** (no item layers and no hand marks for monsters).
- **Frame standard:** the same 512 px frames (512 x 640 for the hop), ground line 42 px above the bottom, transparent PNG, no effects, no shadow. The real size on the stage comes from a scale per monster in the page (planned, not built: `docs/DEV_NOTES.md`, "Stage size and headroom"). A monster wider than tall (a slime, a wolf) is sized by its WIDTH in the cell, not by a 408 px height.
- **The five defaults are drawn once and the other elements are re-skins** (the Assassin / Spellblade method, `docs/RESKIN_PILOT_PLAN.md`: pose guides from the first strips, `check_reskin.py`, `reskin_strip.py`), so all variants of a family share the same timing, path and hit frame. For slimes the shape is a dome with a wavy skirt, so the guides are easy.
- **A monster's own elemental parts are drawn IN its sprites** (the user, 2026-11-07: a bare body with code effects would look too simple; the user's own sheets have them painted in). THE RULE: what is CRISP and OPAQUE goes into the sprite (flame crown, embers as small solid flames, smoke as solid outlined cartoon clouds, crystals, spikes, hairline arcs, star sparkles, ink wisps); what is SOFT, GLOWING or TRANSPARENT stays out of it (glow, halo, bloom, light rays, heat haze, frost mist, see-through smoke), because the sheets are keyed from a flat magenta and a soft edge cannot be separated from it (a pink halo; the project's old lesson: no violet or glow on keyed pictures). The soft parts come later as separate painted layers drawn additively (like the mage's skill textures; the auras `fire_aura`, `frost_aura`, `lightning_aura`, `dark_aura`, `holy_aura` already exist in `source/assets/fx/`) plus our usual code effects (hit flash, statuses). Wins of drawing the effects in: they flicker and drift differently in every frame for free, and the death can put the fire out and let the smoke rise. Costs: the cutters scale by figure height, so flames of different heights would resize the body: cut slimes by the width of their base (a small tool change); the re-skin checker's silhouette test sees the crown, so judge those frames on the body. Untested until the first idle sheet is cut: if the smoke or flames key badly, fall back to the bare body (version 1 in the prompt file) and layer the effects.

## The slimes (act 1, stages 1 to 20)

The user's picture `inbox/Slime_variations.png` (1024 x 572, copy and per-element crops in `Animations/Slime/source/`) shows five elemental slimes: **fire, ice, lightning, dark, holy** (the same five elements as the auras). The user's plan is to build the animations from them.

**Verdict: usable as the LOOK, not as pixels.**
- Good: one consistent painted style with the characters (thick dark outlines, glossy), the same dome-and-skirt body in all five, each element readable in silhouette and colour, strong faces (fire angry, ice frowning, lightning jagged, dark glowing violet, holy smiling).
- Not usable as sprites: (1) each body is only about 170 x 110 px (the whole slime about 190 x 190 with its effects), soft when enlarged; a slime in a 512 px cell needs a body about 350 px wide, so 2x. (2) The soft effects (smoke haze, glows, rays) are semi-transparent over grey and cannot be keyed off; the crisp ones can be redrawn crisp. (3) Front view, one pose each.
- Which parts go into the sprite (crisp, opaque) and which are left for layers (soft, glowing) is in the element notes of `Animations/Slime/source/slime_fire_body_reference_prompt.txt` (version 2, the user's choice; version 1, the bare body, is its fallback). Two flags: the dark slime is violet, so it needs a green background instead of magenta (cutters use another key colour); the holy slime needs a darker gold outline so a magenta key does not fringe.
- The silhouettes differ a little (ice crystals on the back, lightning spikes, fire's flame crown is an effect and goes to code), which the re-skin method handles (the user's looks were never recolours before either).

**Plan:** (1) a reference picture of the FIRE slime WITH its crisp flames and solid smoke (my recommendation for the first one: its dome is the plainest body and its face can do an attack; the flames and smoke are the part the user wants drawn in), prompt `Animations/Slime/source/slime_fire_body_reference_prompt.txt`, result to `inbox/slime_fire_reference.png`; (2) its five default animations as sheets (idle 6 frames, attack 8 with a path, hit 6, death 6, dodge 6 with a path); (3) the other four elements as re-skins; (4) the page: a monster slot in place of the goblin block, a scale per monster; (5) the special animations.
