# Recolour: hair and skin of the Warrior, Mage, Rogue and Cleric (2026-11-24, all four classes since 2026-11-25)

The test page has a **Recolour** row in the Fight panel (shown for the Warrior, the Mage, the Rogue and the Cleric; hidden for the Assassin and the Spellblade, which have no masks yet): a colour box and a row of swatches for the hair, the same for the skin, and an "Original colours" button. **Each character has its own colours**, remembered in the browser (`localStorage`, key `wvg-recolor2` = `{warrior: {hair, skin}, mage: ..., rogue: ..., cleric: ...}`; the first version's key `wvg-recolor` held the Warrior's one choice and moves in as his), painted before the idle shows, so a reload keeps them and switching character brings that character's colours back. The first skin swatch is the character's own skin; the boxes show its own colours when nothing is changed.

## What was decided before (2026-11-05)

The hair mask tool (`source/tools/hair_mask.py`) was adapted for our characters and the user said to just take notes: nothing used the masks, the Warrior's gaps stayed as they were, and FUTURE characters would get a hair colour that is easy to select (a saturated colour nothing else on the figure shares). On 2026-11-24 the user asked for the recolour tool in the page, and for a skin mask too. Both were built for the Warrior first; on 2026-11-25 the user asked for the same on the Mage, the Rogue and the Cleric (below, "The other three classes").

## How it works

1. **Masks next to every Warrior strip:** `<strip>.hair-mask.png` (made by `hair_mask.py`) and `<strip>.skin-mask.png` (made by `skin_mask.py`, new): grey PNGs the size of the strip, white = hair / skin, grey = partly. `source/tools/build.py` inlines them (`default_anims(..., masks=True)`, for the Warrior, the Mage, the Rogue and the Cleric) as `hairMask` / `skinMask`.
2. **The page paints a recoloured copy of each strip** (`rcRender` in `warrior-vs-goblin-test.src.html`) when a colour is chosen, and makes it the pack's picture (`pk.src` / `pk.img`; the originals stay in `pk.src0` / `pk.img0`). So the sprite, the HUD portrait, the hands drawn over the swords (`buildHand` finds the hand on the ORIGINAL pixels and draws the recoloured ones) and the path editor's ghosts all show it.
   - **Hair is colourised:** every masked pixel takes the chosen colour at its own brightness relative to the hair's average (`RC_REF.hairV`), so shading and highlights stay (darker pixels darker, brighter ones lighter).
   - **Skin is tinted:** every masked pixel's channels are multiplied by chosen colour / his average skin colour (`RC_REF.skin`), so the muscle shading stays.
   - Both are blended by the mask's weight (the masks are blurred a little when loaded so the recolour fades out at the edge).
3. **Cache and background work:** the copies are cached per colour pair as blob URLs; after a change the loaded packs are recoloured first, then the page prepares the character's other strips one at a time in the background (`rcWarm`, over `CHARACTERS[curChar].anims`), so a skill starts without a wait. A strip that loads later (a skill) is recoloured in `loadPack` before it is used.
4. **`RC_CFG`** (in the page, per character: `ref` and the name of the first skin swatch): the character's own average hair colour, its average brightness (`hairV`) and its average skin colour, measured on the idle strip with the masks (mean over the mask pixels with weight above one half; Warrior hair (47, 46, 47), V 0.19, skin (179, 123, 95), re-measured 2026-11-25 when his skin mask grew; Mage hair (175, 135, 86), V 0.69, skin (210, 159, 140); Rogue hair (101, 37, 50), V 0.40, skin (165, 116, 82); Cleric hair (37, 109, 65), V 0.43, skin (209, 160, 110)). `RC` and `RC_REF` always point at the character on stage (`rcSelect(key)`, called by `switchCharacter` before the strips load). Choosing exactly the reference colours changes nothing. If a character's art or masks change, measure again.

## The masks

**Skin** (`skin_mask.py`, profile `warrior`; fine-tuned 2026-11-25 after the user saw that the skin around the hair and the ears was missing). Four layers, each one added on top of the one before:
1. **Core (strict):** warm pixels (hue 0 to 36, and 348 to 360 for the far hand's pinker shading; saturation 0.25 to 0.8) that are bright (value 0.45 and up). The core is opened with a 5 x 5 square so the copper trim and the armour's edge lines vanish (the leather bracers are darker and are out too); blobs under 24 px go (it was 60: the far fist is cut into small pieces by its knuckle lines). Then the darker shading is grown back 3 px over warm pixels (value 0.28 and up).
2. **Body growth (loose):** from that skin, up to 8 more steps over warm pixels with saturation 0.2 and up and value 0.33 and up, after a 3 x 3 opening (thin lines go). This fills the far fist, the shadowed arms and the thumb between the bright patches. The value floor 0.33 is what keeps the brown leather bracers (value under 0.3) out.
3. **Head rule (loosest), only within 30 px of the HAIR** (so the hair mask must exist: run `hair_mask.py` first; the skin tool reads `<strip>.hair-mask.png`): warm pixels down to value 0.14 (the ear, the temple under the hair, the neck's shadow), up to 14 steps from the skin so far, again after a 3 x 3 opening.
4. **Bridging (head only):** a loose blob of at least 14 px that lies within 4 px of the skin joins it even across a dark outline line (the lit brow ridge over the eye, the throat). Newly grown pixels get 0.85 to 0.9, the core 1.0; the mask is softened a little.
Face, ears, neck, bare arms, brow ridge and fists are skin; eyes, the dark outline and the hair are not. Contact sheets: `source/skin_check/warrior_<animation>.png` (skin painted cyan).

**Hair** (`hair_mask.py`, profile `warrior`): the face finder takes the highest skin blob as the face and the hair is the dark blob that lies above it. It fails when his arms are above his head, when he lies down, and in a few other poses (no hair at all on 16 frames), so those frames have a **head seed**: a point on the hair read from the picture, in `WARRIOR_SEEDS` (`{folder: {frame: (x, y)}}`, frames counted from 1, cell coordinates); the hair there is the neutral dark pixels (his hair is nearly colourless, his armour a little blue, his leather brown) connected to the seed within a head's radius (`hair_from_seed`). Seeded frames: death 5 6, execute 3, glacial_blade 4 5, jump_attack 4 5 6, molten_cleave 5 6, run 4, static_bash 10 15 17, sunder_strike 2 3, dodge 3. Every frame of every strip now has hair (about 900 to 2900 px). Contact sheets: `source/hair_check/warrior_<animation>.png` (hair painted green).

To remake them after a strip changes: `python source/tools/hair_mask.py --all --char warrior` and THEN `python source/tools/skin_mask.py --all --char warrior` (the skin tool reads the hair masks), then rebuild. A new or re-cut strip with a head in a new place needs its seed if the hair comes out empty.

## The other three classes (2026-11-25)

All three use the same engine; what differs is the masks. Skin profiles are in `skin_mask.py` (`PROFILES["mage" | "rogue" | "cleric"]`), hair profiles were already in `hair_mask.py`. Run `hair_mask.py --all --char <c>` first and `skin_mask.py --all --char <c>` second. Contact sheets: `source/skin_check/<char>_<animation>.png` and `source/hair_check/`.

**New switches in `skin_mask.py`** (the Warrior's numbers are unchanged; his masks were checked byte for byte after the edit): `no_hair` (what the hair mask covers is never skin, because blonde hair and brows are warm too), `hue_margin` (how far past the hue window the shading may reach), `body_hue_min/max` (the hue range of the loose growth), `head_wrap` (the red hue that the head rule may use: the Warrior's pinkish shading, but NOT the Rogue's crimson scarf), `min_peak_val` (a piece of mask whose 90th-percentile brightness is below it is dropped: boot trim, a leather thigh strap), `body_bridge*` (a lit patch of a fist that a knuckle line cuts off joins, size-limited so a whole tabard never does).

- **Mage** (pale peach skin, hue 8 to 28): the skin mask is clean on all 37 frames (face, ears, neck, both hands). Her HAIR mask needed work: in the cast, dodge, hit, run and death strips the lower braid is pinkish-brown, outside the hair's hue window, and the old chain stopped at her shoulder, so a recoloured head had a blonde braid on about a third of the frames. `hair_mask.py` has a **braid pass** now (profile key `braid`): blobs of a wider warm colour (hue 5 to 62, saturation 0.38 and up) that are thick (a braid is, a trim line is not) and within 21 px of the hair so far join it, link by link, in two sweeps (a 3 x 3 and a 5 x 5 opening: the second cuts the braid loose from the robe's gold collar trim it touches); a first link up to 30 px away counts only if it is really thick and within 75 px of the face (the belt pouch is not). Still not covered: the braid's tip in cast frame 4 and a sliver of the braid in cast frame 7 (small); the idle masks did not change.
- **Rogue** (dark tan skin, hue 10 to 34): faces and hands are covered on all 103 frames; the crimson scarf, the brown boots and the leather straps are not. Her HAIR mask is the old one (about 85% of her hair) except two frames where the chin rule cut a head that is thrown back with a flat line (hit 2, death 3): `FRAME_OVERRIDES` in `hair_mask.py` switches the chin rule off there. **Strands pass (2026-11-25, the user asked to fix the old known gap):** the dark burgundy strands at her nape (behind and under the ear) and the lock that hangs behind her jaw on the far side have the SAME colour as the scarf's dark folds, so no colour rule alone can take them; `hair_mask.py` now has a `strands` pass for the Rogue that decides by colour AND place: burgundy only (hue 325 to 358, saturation 0.5 and up, value 0.10 to 0.46, and not skin, which has hue 10 to 34), not within 8 px of the scarf (every big blob of the hair's colour that is not the hair, filled in and widened), behind the face (measured from the face's middle towards the hair's middle; in front only up to chin level), at most 40 px from the face's middle, within 22 px of the hair, thick (a 5 x 5 opening: a strand is 5 to 10 px thick, the hood's crimson rim line is not), and above the chin line on upright heads. The profile numbers are the `strands=dict(...)` of `PROFILES["rogue"]`. Result: the nape and the far lock take the new colour on the idle, run, hit, dodge and the skills; the scarf, the hood rim and the skin are untouched (checked on all 103 frames with the added pixels painted yellow). Small leftovers: a few specks of the lock's thin lower tip, and in a few Static Stiletto frames a thin line goes a little down the neck. **The ear (2026-11-25, the user: the hair colour also changed her ear):** the ear is a small tan shape inside the hair, and the hair mask's hole filling had covered it. `hair_mask.py` has an `ears` step for the Rogue (`PROFILES["rogue"]["ears"]`): tan blobs (hue 12 to 36, saturation 0.3 and up, value 0.38 and up, 6 to 450 px, grown 1 px for the lit rim and the shading) that lie inside the hair mask are cut out of it; `skin_mask.py` has an `ear_skin` rule that takes a small tan blob lying against the hair (at most 14 px deep into it) as skin, because the strict rules cannot find a thin, half-shaded ear. Checked on the six idle frames (the ear and the neck sliver under it are skin, the hair around them is not) and on the run, hit and dodge heads; in the page, a hair-only change leaves her ear the colour of her face. Only the Rogue has this step; the Warrior, Mage and Cleric masks are byte-identical to before.
- **Cleric** (tan skin, hue 16 to 32 strict; the cream tabard starts at hue 35): the loose rules reach up to hue 41 to 43 because lit skin is yellower, but only near the hair or right next to the skin (the tabard would leak otherwise); the tabard, the gold trim and the boot trim are clean on all 80 frames. His HAIR mask gets his **eyebrows** now (`brows`: any blob of green, hue 60 to 175, near the face), which were a separate green line before.

Tested in headless Chrome: each class with extreme colours (magenta hair, violet skin; red, blue, blond, white, dark brown) on the idle, attack, dodge, block (the Cleric), run and a whole fight: no console errors, no frame that flashes back to the original hair colour (the frames without recoloured pixels are the Rogue's vanishing skills and the Victory dim). Per-character memory, the migration of the old key and a reload were tested too.

## Limits and ideas

- Tested with extreme colours (magenta hair, violet and dark-brown skin) on all six skills, the idle, dodge, block, run, hit and death, with the shield and with two swords: no leaks (the hair patch is never bigger than a head) and no frame without hair. Very pale or very dark skin colours are approximations by a per-channel gain (a very bright target saturates highlights).
- Wired: the Warrior, the Mage, the Rogue and the Cleric. The Spellblade has hair masks but no skin masks and the Assassin has neither; to add a class: a skin profile in `skin_mask.py` (and a hair profile in `hair_mask.py` if it has none), `masks=True` for its call in `build.py`, its entry in `RC_CFG` (measure the idle with the masks) and its name in the panel's `data-chars`.
- Eyes, lips, the bracers, the armour and the weapons are not recoloured (the orange brow ridge over the eye counts as skin) (an armour or leather colour would be a third mask).
