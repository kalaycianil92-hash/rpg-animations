# Gemini prompts that worked

Three animations were made with the prompts below (the idle and jump attack were made in earlier chats and their prompts were not kept). Each produced a usable sheet in one or two tries. To reuse one for a new character, change the CHARACTER paragraph and keep everything else; adapt the pose list.

**How to use:** attach the reference image(s) to the Gemini message first, then paste the prompt. If you can set an aspect ratio, pick 3:2 (six square panels). Generate several and keep the best. Save the sheet in `inbox/` (see `PLAYBOOK.md` for cutting).

**The standing blocks** every prompt contains, in this order: what to make (grid, order, the animation in one sentence), CHARACTER (copied from the reference, "do not redesign anything"), VIEW (side view, facing right, where the attacker is), NO WEAPON, SAME SCALE AND FLOOR, ARMS (near arm in front, far arm behind), THE SIX FRAMES (a pose per frame), BACKGROUND AND LAYOUT (flat magenta, no grid lines, margin, nothing crosses a panel edge, no effects or text).

**For a new character:** replace the CHARACTER paragraph. If the character holds a weapon in the animation, keep the hands as empty fists anyway (the weapon is a layer); for casting poses with open hands, say so in the frame list, and the hand marks hide the item on those frames.

**Lessons**
- Gemini follows pose lists and "first and last frame equal the reference stance" well.
- It drew the figure about 65% of the panel height without being asked; asking for 60% left headroom for hops.
- It leaves a faint purple glow along outlines (handled by the cutters' despill options) and drifts the figure size by a few percent between panels.
- Every extra thing Gemini makes counts against the image limit. While building the mage's idle it also made 4 GIFs and 3 more PNGs. Start the prompt with an OUTPUT line (below), end it with the same reminder, and avoid words that invite a GIF ("animation", "played in half a second", "loop"): describe the panels as "key poses" of a sprite sheet instead.
- Emotional close-up frames (the stunned and the hit face in the mage's death sheet) came out about 1.5 times too large, and the top row of that sheet was cut flat at 85% of the panel height (the "floor" line), which sliced the legs off two frames and a boot off a third. Fix in the prompt: give the head size as a share of the panel ("the head from hair to chin is only about 10% of the panel height"), the whole body as about 55 to 60% for tilted poses, the floor lower (about 78%), and say "the whole body from the top of the head to the soles of both boots is visible in every panel, nothing cropped". When a sheet is half good, do not redo it: regenerate only the bad poses (a whole sheet of takes of one pose costs the same as one image) and join the good frames.
- Size drift is bigger than "2 to 4 percent" when a sheet mixes standing and lying poses: in the mage's death sheet the falling and landing frames came out about 1.25 times too big and one falling frame 0.85 times. Check each cut with the face area and the boots (see `--scale-mult` in `PLAYBOOK.md`) instead of trusting the eye.
- If it copies the old outfit when you use a pose reference, reply: "Redo it. The character in all panels must wear <outfit> from the second image. Do not copy any clothing from the first image."

## Hit reaction (6 frames, 3 x 2, attach the idle strip)

```text
Create ONE sprite sheet image: a grid of 3 columns x 2 rows, six equal square panels, read left to right, top row first, then the bottom row. It is a "hit reaction" animation: the character is struck from the front, recoils, staggers and recovers. Six key frames, played in half a second.

CHARACTER: the exact same character as in the attached reference image: young muscular fighter, spiky black hair, tan skin, dark navy segmented plate armor with copper trim (shoulder pauldrons, chest plate, belt), brown leather bracers, dark trousers, navy plated shin guards, black boots. Keep the same painted game-sprite style, thick dark outlines, colors, proportions and face. Do not redesign anything.

VIEW: strict side view, the character faces RIGHT in every panel. The attacker is off-screen to the right, so the blow comes from the front and throws the character's head and upper body back toward the LEFT.

NO WEAPON, NO SHIELD: both hands are empty, loosely clenched fists (no open splayed fingers).

SAME SCALE: draw the character at exactly the same size in all six panels, the same standing height as in the reference. Do not zoom in or out between panels. The rear foot (the left foot) stays on the ground at the same spot and the same floor height in every panel, as if on an invisible floor line. Only the body, head, arms and front foot move. The sprite must not travel across the panel.

ARMS: the far arm (the one farther from the viewer) always stays behind the torso, and the near arm (the one closer to the viewer) always stays in front of the torso. Never swap them between panels.

THE SIX FRAMES:
1. Impact. Upright guard stance exactly like the reference: full height, fists up, both feet planted. The blow has just landed: eyes squeezed shut, teeth gritted, head just starting to snap back.
2. Peak recoil. Head and upper body thrown far back to the left, chin up, mouth open in pain, back arched, arms flung out and down, front foot lifted slightly off the ground, knees slightly bent.
3. Stagger. Still leaning back but starting to recover, rear leg braced hard, front foot touching down again, one arm swinging forward for balance, wincing.
4. Recover. Torso coming forward again, head dropping to face right, knees bent, fists coming back up toward guard height, grimacing.
5. Settle. Nearly upright, slightly hunched forward, fists almost back in guard, eyes open and glaring at the enemy.
6. Back to guard. The same upright guard stance as the reference and as frame 1, neutral determined face.

BACKGROUND AND LAYOUT: one flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) background filling the whole image, identical in every pixel. No gradient, floor, ground line, cast shadow, glow or vignette. No grid lines or borders between panels. Leave about 10% empty margin around the character in each panel; the whole character, hands and feet included, must stay fully inside its own panel and never touch or cross a panel edge. No text, numbers, labels, motion lines, impact stars, blood, sweat drops or any other effects. No magenta or pink on the character itself.
```

Cut with the hit command in `PLAYBOOK.md` (frames 1 and 6 are the full-height guard stance, so `--fit 1,6`).

## Death (6 frames, 3 x 2, attach the old death strip first, then the new character's idle)

This one copies the poses of an existing strip from an older character onto the new one. The old strip is in `Animations/Warrior/death/source/old_character_death_pose_reference.png`.

```text
You get two reference images.
The FIRST attached image is a strip of 6 animation frames of a fighter being hit, falling backwards and lying on the ground. Use it ONLY as a pose reference.
The SECOND attached image shows the character I want.

TASK: redraw the 6 frames of the first image with the character of the second image. Each frame must keep the exact pose of the same frame in the first image: the same body angle, head tilt, facing, arm and leg positions, expression and camera (strict side view). Only the character changes. Output them as ONE sprite sheet: a grid of 3 columns x 2 rows, six equal square panels, read left to right, top row first. Panels 1, 2, 3 are on the top row and panels 4, 5, 6 on the bottom row, in the same order as the frames of the first image.

CHARACTER: the exact character of the second image: young muscular fighter, spiky black hair, tan skin, dark navy segmented plate armor with copper trim (shoulder pauldrons, chest plate, belt), brown leather bracers, dark trousers, navy plated shin guards and black boots. Keep the same painted game-sprite style, thick dark outlines, colors, proportions and face. Ignore the outfit in the first image: no bare chest, no bandage wraps, no bare feet. The armor, bracers, shin guards and boots must be worn in every panel, also when he is falling and lying down. Both hands are empty fists. No weapon, no shield.

DIRECTION: while standing he faces RIGHT, and the blow throws him backwards toward the LEFT, so his head ends up on the left and his feet on the right, exactly like the first image.

THE SIX FRAMES:
1. Stunned. Upright guard stance, feet wide apart and planted, chin raised and head tilted back so he looks up, fists up in front of him.
2. The hit. The same stance, face in a pained grimace, teeth gritted, eyes squeezed shut, chest pushed out, fists clenched in front.
3. Falling backwards. Body tilted back about 40 degrees from vertical, head at the upper left, feet at the lower right just at the ground, legs together and slightly bent, near arm trailing down behind him on the left, far arm flung out to the upper right, looking up.
4. Falling further. Body tilted about 60 degrees from vertical, head far to the left, legs to the lower right, near arm hanging down on the left, far arm stretched out to the right, almost horizontal.
5. Landing. Lying on his back on the left with his head on the ground, torso on the ground, knees bent and both legs up in the air (one leg raised high, the other bent out to the right), the far arm raised with a fist up, the near hand near his chest.
6. Lying still. Flat on his back, head on the left, body horizontal, legs stretched out to the right and slightly bent at the knees, arms resting loosely at his sides, eyes closed, limp.

SCALE AND POSITION: one single scale in all six panels, the same as in the first image: standing, the character is about 65% of the panel height; the lying figure in panel 6 is slightly shorter than that standing height is tall, and fits inside the panel width with a margin. In every panel the lowest point of the figure rests on the same floor height (about 85% down the panel). Keep each figure horizontally centered in its panel like the first image. Keep the near arm in front of the body and the far arm behind it in every panel.

BACKGROUND AND LAYOUT: one flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) background filling the whole image, identical in every pixel. No gradient, floor, ground line, cast shadow, glow or vignette. No grid lines or borders between panels. The whole character must stay fully inside its own panel and never touch or cross a panel edge. No text, numbers, labels, dust, motion lines, impact stars, blood or any other effects. No magenta or pink on the character itself.
```

Cut with the death command in `PLAYBOOK.md`. For a new character you could instead describe the six poses in words (stunned, hit, two falling frames, landing, lying) and skip the pose reference.

## Dodge (6 frames, 3 x 2, attach the idle strip)

A quick hop up and back, in place (the page slides the character back and the path file moves it).

```text
Create ONE sprite sheet image: a grid of 3 columns x 2 rows, six equal square panels, read left to right, top row first, then the bottom row. It is a "dodge" animation: the character reacts to an incoming attack by quickly hopping up and back, lands, and returns to his guard. Six key frames, played in about half a second.

CHARACTER: the exact same character as in the attached reference image: young muscular fighter, spiky black hair, tan skin, dark navy segmented plate armor with copper trim (shoulder pauldrons, chest plate, belt), brown leather bracers, dark trousers, navy plated shin guards, black boots. Keep the same painted game-sprite style, thick dark outlines, colors, proportions and face. Do not redesign anything.

VIEW: strict side view, the character faces RIGHT in every panel. The attacker is off-screen to the right, so the hop goes up and slightly back toward the LEFT.

NO WEAPON, NO SHIELD: both hands are empty, tight fists, held in a guard near the chin and chest in every panel.

SAME SCALE AND FLOOR: the character is drawn at exactly the same size in all six panels, the same standing height as in the reference. Do not zoom in or out between panels. The floor is at the same height in every panel, about 80% down the panel, and the standing figure is about 60% of the panel height, which leaves room above the head for the hop. The hop goes mostly UP; the character stays at about the same horizontal position in every panel (the hips do not travel across the panel, at most a small lean and drift to the left at the peak).

ARMS: the far arm (the one farther from the viewer) always stays behind the torso, and the near arm (the one closer to the viewer) always stays in front of the torso. Never swap them between panels.

THE SIX FRAMES:
1. React. Upright guard stance exactly like the reference: full height, both feet planted on the floor, fists up. Eyes wide and focused on the enemy, weight just beginning to shift onto the rear leg.
2. Coil. Both feet still on the floor, knees bent, torso leaning back about 10 degrees, weight on the rear leg, front foot only on its toes, fists tight at the chin.
3. Peak of the hop. BOTH FEET OFF THE FLOOR by about 20% of the character's height, knees tucked slightly, torso leaning back about 15 degrees, head back, fists at the chin, eyes on the enemy.
4. Coming down. Still in the air, feet about 8% of the character's height above the floor, legs extending down with the toes pointing toward the floor, torso leaning back about 10 degrees, fists still in guard.
5. Landing. Both feet back on the floor, knees deeply bent to absorb the impact, torso leaning slightly forward, fists in guard, looking at the enemy.
6. Recover. The same upright guard stance as the reference and as frame 1: fully upright, fists raised, neutral determined face.

BACKGROUND AND LAYOUT: one flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) background filling the whole image, identical in every pixel. No gradient, floor, ground line, cast shadow, glow or vignette. No grid lines or borders between panels. Leave about 10% empty margin around the character in each panel; the whole character, hands and feet included, must stay fully inside its own panel and never touch or cross a panel edge. No text, numbers, labels, dust, motion lines, speed lines, afterimages or any other effects. No magenta or pink on the character itself.
```

Cut with the dodge command in `PLAYBOOK.md` (frames 3 and 4 are airborne: about 81 px and 32 px up in the finished strip). Gemini followed the heights well.

## Mage (the elf): the blocks that worked

The look came from one prompt (single full-body figure, the warrior idle frame attached as a style reference only, near and far hands described, no left or right) and then an edit prompt in the same Gemini chat ("redraw this exact character, change only the head angle and the fist hand"). Gemini decided which hand holds the fist: the near arm. The reference image is `inbox/reference_image.png` (move it into `Animations/Mage/` when the inbox is cleaned). For every sheet attach only that image, ask for 3:2, and reuse these blocks word for word:

```text
OUTPUT: exactly ONE static image, a PNG sprite sheet, and nothing else. Do NOT create a GIF, an animation, a video, a preview, extra images, alternate versions or a second attempt. No other files.
```

```text
CHARACTER: the exact same character as in the attached reference image: a female elf mage with long pointed ears, fair skin, green eyes and a calm, focused expression, long golden-blonde hair tied into one long braid hanging down her back, a deep royal-blue robe with gold trim and a light-blue front panel, a high collar, wide trumpet-shaped sleeves with gold-trimmed cuffs, a brown leather belt with a small pouch, the robe reaching mid-calf with a front slit over dark fitted leggings and brown leather boots. Keep the same painted game-sprite style, thick dark outlines, colors, proportions, face and three-quarter head angle. Do not redesign anything.
```

```text
HANDS (identical in every panel, exactly as in the reference): the near hand (the one closest to the viewer) is a loosely CLOSED FIST held in front of her belt, as if holding a vertical pole that is not drawn. The far hand is OPEN with the palm facing UP and relaxed fingers, held out in front of her to the right. Never swap the two hands between panels. Both hands are bare and empty: nothing in the fist, nothing on or above the open palm.
```

The idle sheet (3 x 2, figure about 65% of the panel, floor about 85% down, both feet planted, "very small movements, a gentle breathing cycle") came out with the whole upper body bobbing about 10 px; `damp_motion.py` (see `PLAYBOOK.md`) halved that. The mage's hit sheet came out right on the first try: the warrior's hit prompt above with the blocks here (plus ARMS: near arm in front, far arm behind, and "the near hand is always a tight closed fist, the far hand always open"), the OUTPUT line at the start and a one-line reminder at the end, "key poses of a hit reaction" instead of "animation", and these frames: 1 impact (the ready stance, grimace), 2 peak recoil (head and body thrown back to the left, near fist pulled to the hip, far arm flung out with the hand spread, front foot lifted), 3 stagger (rear leg braced, front foot down, far arm swinging forward), 4 recover (torso coming forward, near fist back to belt height), 5 settle (nearly upright, glaring), 6 back to ready (the same stance as the reference, calm face). One flaw: in frame 5 the far hand came out as a fist. Cut with `--height 406` and the command in `PLAYBOOK.md` with `--heel-x 230`.

## Firebolt painted textures (one sheet, 3 x 2, no reference image, ask for 3:2)

Not an animation: six still pictures on pure black that the page animates (scale, rotate, flicker between the two heads, cross-fade the three impact stages) and adds with additive blending, so nothing is cut out. Cut with `source/tools/cut_fx_sheet.py` into `source/assets/fx/`; the page then offers "Look: Painted" next to "Code" in the Firebolt panel. Panels: 1 and 2 fireball head (flying RIGHT, core at the right end, different tongues), 3 flame trail (a long streak, bright at the right end), 4 to 6 impact (first instant, expanding, fading). First result (2026-10-30): Gemini made a 3 x 3 sheet of 1024 x 1024 with white divider lines and 9 pictures (the pure black background was perfect); it was cut with `cut_fx_sheet.py --cols 3 --rows 3 "--names=fb_head_a,fb_head_b,fb_trail,fb_burst_1,fb_burst_2,-,fb_burst_1b,fb_burst_2b,fb_fade"` (quote or `=` the names, they start with a dash). The pictures are about 300 px wide, enough for the sizes the page draws. The user did not like those cartoon-style pictures (especially the meteor-like dying fire) and generated a second sheet (1536 x 1024, a clean 3 x 2, no dividers, more realistic painterly flames, a rising-flames third impact stage): that one is in use, cut with `cut_fx_sheet.py --cols 3 --rows 2 --min 40 --pad 12` (the default names). Lesson: ask for a realistic painterly fire, not a cartoon one, if you want it to sit next to the code-made glows.

```text
OUTPUT: exactly ONE static image, a PNG texture sheet, and nothing else. Do NOT create a GIF, an animation, a video, a preview, extra images, alternate versions or a second attempt. No other files.

Create a texture sheet of FIRE effects for a 2D fantasy RPG game: a grid of 3 columns x 2 rows, six equal square panels, read left to right, top row first, then the bottom row.

BACKGROUND (very important): the whole image is PURE BLACK (#000000), perfectly flat and identical in every pixel. The effects will be added over a game scene with additive blending, so black means transparent. THE SIX PANELS:

5. Impact, expanding. A larger fire explosion: orange flame tongues bursting outward in all directions around a bright core, with a few small flying embers, centered, about 80% of the panel.
6. Impact, fading. A dying fireball: ragged red and orange flame wisps rising and dissolving, a few glowing embers, centered, about 90% of the panel, dimmer than panel 5.

LAYOUT: each effect is centered in its own panel with at least 8% pure black margin around it and never touches or crosses the panel edge.

FINAL REMINDER: output ONE single static PNG image only. No GIF, no extra images.
```

## Deep Freeze painted textures (one sheet, 3 x 2, no reference image, ask for 3:2)

The code-drawn Deep Freeze (aura, climbing ice, translucent block, shatter) was judged "not bad" but the user wants it to look better, so this sheet is for a painted look like Firebolt's (one "Look" select: Code or Painted). Same method as the Firebolt textures: six stills on pure black that the page adds with additive blending (black = transparent), cut with `source/tools/cut_fx_sheet.py --cols 3 --rows 2 --min 40 --pad 12 --names fz_block,fz_block_cracked,fz_aura,fz_ground,fz_crystals,fz_shatter` into `source/assets/fx/`. How the page will use them: 1 the giant block (stretched to the block's box and revealed from the ground up behind the code's glowing ragged front, so the climbing still works; the target shows through because its inside is dark), 2 the cracked block (cross-faded in for the last 0.25 s before the shatter), 3 the frost aura column (grows and fades in round the target during the build-up, mirrored for variety), 4 the frost on the ground (grows under the target), 5 the crystals along the foot of the block, 6 the shatter burst (with the code's slabs and chips on top). Lessons carried over from the Firebolt sheets: ask for a realistic painterly look (the cartoon sheet was rejected), say it is a clean 3 x 2 with no divider lines, black must be pure and flat, no haze or glow across the panel, and ONE image only. Result (2026-10-31): the user generated two sheets; the first (1536 x 1024, a clean 3 x 2, pure black, a faceted translucent block with a dark inside, a real frost aura with snowflakes) was chosen and is in use, cut with `cut_fx_sheet.py --cols 3 --rows 2 --min 40 --pad 12 "--names=fz_block,fz_block_cracked,fz_aura,fz_ground,fz_crystals,fz_shatter"` (sizes about 360 to 500 px). The second (1024 x 1024) had grey divider lines, an irregular panel layout (the burst spanned two rows), a block that read as a glass pane in a frame, and smaller panels. Both are kept in `source/assets/fx/originals/`.

```text
OUTPUT: exactly ONE static image, a PNG texture sheet, and nothing else. Do NOT create a GIF, an animation, a video, a preview, extra images, alternate versions or a second attempt. No other files.

Create a texture sheet of ICE effects for a 2D fantasy RPG game: a grid of 3 columns x 2 rows, six equal square panels (the whole image is 3:2), read left to right, top row first, then the bottom row.

BACKGROUND (very important): the whole image is PURE BLACK (#000000), perfectly flat and identical in every pixel. The effects will be added over a game scene with additive blending, so black means transparent and everything that is not black adds light. No gradient, no vignette, no dark-grey or blue haze, no glow spreading across the panel (a glow may reach at most 5% beyond the object and must be pure black well before the panel edge), no outlines, no borders, no divider lines or frames between the panels.

STYLE: realistic hand-painted fantasy game ice, painterly brush shading, the same look as a painted RPG sprite: believable crystalline ice with sharp facets, refraction streaks, a bright rim light on every edge, tiny frozen bubbles and frost specks. Not cartoon, not flat vector, not photo. Colours: white-cyan (#E9F8FF) highlights and edges, light blue (#96D6FF), mid blue (#4A96EE), deep blue (#224EB0) only in thin shadow lines. No characters, no animals, no ground, no sky, no snow landscape, no text, no numbers.

IMPORTANT FOR THE ICE BLOCKS (panels 1 and 2): they are see-through. A character will stand inside them and must show through, so the INSIDE of the ice is very dark (near black, deep navy at most) with only soft blue refraction streaks; the brightness is concentrated on the edges, the facet lines and a few highlights.

THE SIX PANELS:
1. Giant ice block. A tall upright slab of solid ice seen straight from the front, about twice as tall as it is wide, filling about 90% of the panel height and centered. Bright white-cyan rim light along all edges and along the inner facet planes.
```

## Writing a prompt for a new animation

1. Start from the closest prompt above. Change the one-sentence description, the VIEW paragraph (where the attacker or target is), and the frame list.
2. Give each frame a body state in plain words and a number where height or angle matters ("both feet off the floor by about 20% of the character's height", "tilted back about 40 degrees").
3. State what must not move (the rear foot for in-place moves) and what must be equal (size, floor height).
4. For a move that returns to the idle, make the last frame (and the first when it starts from guard) "exactly the idle stance".
5. Ask for the grid that fits the frame count: 6 frames 3 x 2, 8 frames 4 x 2, 4 frames 2 x 2, 9 frames 3 x 3.
