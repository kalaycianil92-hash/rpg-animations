# Developer notes

For whoever works on the test page, ports it to the game, or extends it to a second character. Read `README.md` first.

## Decisions and why

- **Frame-by-frame strips plus data.** The art is poses; everything that varies (where hands hold items, where a frame stands, how an effect plays) is data in json or parameters in code. That keeps the art reusable and makes it easy to port to any engine.
- **The weapon is a separate layer.** The hands in the art are empty fists, so the same animation works with any weapon, and a weapon can be swapped per animation. The cost is the hand marks (done once per animation in the hand tool) and the hand overlay at the grip.
- **Skill effects are code, not frames.** Lightning, projectiles, auras, hit sparks, shake and damage numbers are procedural (bolt generators, particles, tweens, flashes). They are tintable, resizable, reusable for other skills, and tuned by eye with sliders. Hand-painted textures can go underneath later if the look is too flat next to the painted characters (a Gemini pass for an impact burst or a scorch mark).
- **Hit and death play in place; only attack and dodge travel.** The user's call, after trying them.
- **Engine: Pixi.js recommended** (a 2D WebGL renderer for sprites, effects and filters; it plays strips natively and is lighter than a full engine). The user does not know game engines and trusted the recommendation. The data is engine-neutral, so the choice is reversible.
- **Subtle effects.** In review the user wanted the sky bolt and the target's shock to be the stars; the ground arcs, rings and sparks are kept quiet.

How it works:
- **Frames.** A pack is a strip image; a frame is shown by changing `background-position` of one element. Frames taller than the idle's (jump, dodge: 640 against 512) are sized by their height ratio and anchored at the bottom, so the feet stay on the ground line. The big image URL is assigned only when the pack changes, never per frame.
- **Timing.** `playPose` derives the frame from a clock (`player.clock = {pose, t0, frameMs}`), so timing does not drift. Everything tied to an animation (the path, the dodge, the skill effect) reads that same clock: frame N is on screen at `t0 + (N - 1) * frameMs`. The impact (damage number, effects, hit reaction) fires 40% into the hit frame.
- **Weapons.** Two canvases the size of the current frame sit under and over the body element (`.gear.back`, `.gear.front`) and are redrawn on every frame change from the marks. Item length is a share of the frame width. The hand overlay uses `buildHand` on the strip image pixels (cached per frame and mark).
- **Paths.** The warrior's `transform` is driven from the path every animation frame (`startPath`), with `fxDraw` called right after it so effects that follow him never lag. Attack x is in units of the distance to the target (`strikeReach`), dodge x in character widths (`unitPx`).
- **Dodge in a fight.** `act` starts `runDodge` early enough that the dodge's evade frame is on screen at the moment of the goblin's strike; the attacker's wind-up is lengthened if the dodge needs more lead time.
- **Skill effect.** One canvas over the stage (`#fx`). Layers have a start and end time (absolute ms on the animation's clock) and a draw function; `fxDraw` clears and draws the live ones. `startSkill` adds the blade crackle, the blade glow, the sky strike and the trail from the frame numbers, and returns an object whose `impact()` the fight calls at the hit moment (`impactFx`). The blades' positions come from the hand marks of the frame on screen (`bladeSegs`), converted to stage pixels through the body element's rectangle. Durations scale with the speed buttons.

- **Props flicker in the slow-down, fixed (2026-11-01).** The user saw a small flicker between the floor and the props while the run slowed to a stop. Cause (measured with a per-frame log of the layers' draw positions and a per-frame pixel comparison of three bands of the canvas, in the pane at 1x with dpr 1): every layer was placed at a whole number of pixels (`Math.floor` of its own offset), and in the last second of the glide, when the ground moves less than a pixel a frame (the speed decays exponentially, `bgV` under about 0.2 character widths a second), the floor (speed 100%) and the props (85%) stepped one pixel on DIFFERENT frames (some frames only the props moved, some only the floor, some neither), so the props jittered against the floor they stand on. Stopping the props first would not remove it: any layer stepping alone would do the same. Fix: `bgPaint` fills each layer as a repeating pattern (`createPattern(t, "repeat-x")`, cached on the picture as `im._pat`) translated by the layer's FRACTIONAL offset while anything moves, so a slow glide is smooth; the tile join has no seam; when everything has stopped (`still` = no speed and no goal) each layer snaps to a whole pixel, so the resting picture is sharp (a jump of at most half a pixel on the last frame). Checked: after the fix the change per frame falls steadily for far, props and floor, with no zero frames and no isolated one-pixel ticks in the glide; the floor's tile join deviates 0.3 levels from the expected interpolation, the far picture's 0.25 and the props' at most 3 (the same as at their edges anywhere); no visible blur. The measuring needed a debug copy of the page (a hook after `bgPaint`, a position setter, a switch to draw fractional offsets at rest); it was deleted.

## Testing in the browser pane

The Claude desktop browser pane is usually a hidden page. What that breaks, and the workaround that was used throughout:

1. **Zero-size viewport.** The page may lay out at width 0. Call the pane's `resize_window` with a custom size (1100 x 900), and `resize_window` with preset `desktop` when finished.
2. **`requestAnimationFrame` never fires, and Web Animations never finish.** Before running anything animated, in the page: `window.requestAnimationFrame = cb => setTimeout(() => cb(performance.now()), 16);` and `Element.prototype.animate = function (f, o) { const d = (o && o.duration) || 0; return { finished: new Promise(r => setTimeout(r, d)), cancel() {} }; };`. Timer-driven code (`playPose`) works as is.
3. **Screenshots are stale or time out.** Instead draw what you want to see onto a canvas in the page (the stage background, the warrior's frame from the strip image at the body element's rectangle, the gear canvases, the effects canvas, the goblin box), and post the PNG to a local receiver: `python source/tools/dev/image_receiver.py <folder> 8766`, then `fetch('http://127.0.0.1:8766/?name=shot', {method: 'POST', body: canvas.toDataURL('image/png')})` and open the PNG. Collect several canvases first and post them after the run (posting while the animation runs stalls it).
4. **Internals.** The page keeps everything inside one function. To inspect, build a copy for testing only (never ship it): in `source/dist`, copy the built page to `_dbg.html` and insert before its last `})();` a line such as `window.__dbg = { packs: () => packs, cur: () => curFrame, clock: () => player.clock, blades: bladeSegs, drawGearTo };`. Serve the project with `python -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1` (the page's own file also works from `file://`, but the pane could not open `file://` URLs), open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/source/dist/_dbg.html?v=N` and change `N` each time (the pane caches). Delete the debug copy and stop both servers afterwards.
5. **What to check for a new effect or animation:** console errors (`read_console_messages`), full fights in both scenarios (win and lose) with the new thing on and off, and the composite images at the key moments.

## Gotchas

- Python 3.14 with pillow, numpy and scipy is what the tools were run with on Windows 11. The scripts take plain file paths.
- Always check your cut with `check_strip.py` against the idle: a wrong reference number (the warrior's toe was documented as 336 when it is 426) misaligns an animation by dozens of pixels without anything looking broken, and every hand mark made on it is tied to that position.
- Re-cutting a strip moves the art inside its frame, and the hand marks are fractions of the frame: after any re-cut that shifts the art, shift the marks by the same amount (done for the dodge: every frame's art moved 45 px and every mark's x moved by 45/512; verified by comparing the old and new strips pixel for pixel).
- The hand tool and the test page are single files that hold their images as data; keep the sources (`source/src`, the tool itself) as the truth and rebuild.

## The Warrior's attack pack can hold frames (2026-11-12)

The Thunderclap Guillotine strip (11 frames) holds its crouch (100 ms each) and its apex (300 ms). The `holds` array in its `.placement.json` (extra ms per frame at 100% speed) is read by `registerAnims` into `PRESETS[file].holds`; `atkHolds()` returns it for the loaded attack pack (or null), `playPose("attack", false, holds)` plays with it (the same `holdClock` the mage's Shatter Lance uses), `clkFrames(clk, now)` is the inverse of `clkAt` (time to a frame position, holds included) and drives the path in `startPath`, and the impact time of the preview, of `act` (the fight) and of `startSkill` is `clkAt(clk, hitF, 0.4)`. A pack without holds takes the old uniform-frame route. `slashDraw` (in `startSkill`) draws the swords' stroke. To test without the browser pane: `scratchpad`-style headless Chrome through the DevTools protocol (a node script that loads `http://127.0.0.1:8765/warrior-vs-goblin-test.html`, clicks `#previewAtk`, samples the sprite's `background-position` and the unit's `transform` every 16 ms and takes screenshots) worked on 2026-11-12 where the pane refused to open localhost; the real Chrome renders animation frames normally.

`tcDim` (2026-11-13) darkens the stage behind the characters from the launch to the impact of the Thunderclap Guillotine (panel checkbox `fxDark`, slider `fxDarkAmt`); it is added in `startSkill` when the strike frame is later than frame 2.

Thunderclap painted lightning (2026-11-13): `tcBolt` (a tall texture standing on a point, its top may leave the stage), `tcSleeve` (a texture along a blade, bright end on the tip) and the `F.paint` / `F.aura` strengths (sliders `fxPaint`, `fxAura`) are in `fxParams`; `skyDraw`, `crackleDraw`, `glowDraw` and `impactFx` draw the tc_* textures when `F.paint > 0`; `startSkill` adds the `lightning_aura` layer for the crouch.

The frame path panel (`#pathPanel`) lives in its own `details#pathDetails` under the stage controls since 2026-11-13 (the open state is remembered with the other menus); `#fxEditPath` in the skill panel opens it and switches the editing on. `onPathPackLoaded` applies the preset's hit frame, travel frame and fps even when a saved path of the user's exists, `resetPath` restores the preset path, and `syncJson` writes the pack's `holds`.

The Warrior's near hand (2026-11-13): `nearShield` (select `#nearHand`, remembered as `wvg-warrior-nearhand`) makes `cfgOf` hand out `SHIELD_CFG` (0.3 long, grip 50%, no hand over it) for the near track of any file whose character is "Warrior"; `itemFor` and `bladeSegs` go through `cfgOf`, so a shield draws on the same marks and carries no lightning.
